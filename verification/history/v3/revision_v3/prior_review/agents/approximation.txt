Reconstruction from the manuscript
1. The common residue construction (882–1036) interpolates at alpha_0=0 and alpha_j=H/a_j, with N=Hc, so the pole residues carry the desired square roots theta_j. Clearing denominators in the polynomial ring produces integral Q entries and anchor factors in their columns. The displayed determinant excludes rank loss. The deformed contour gives a beta-integral residual bound. Finally the tuple roots furnish a common approximation vector; the same chosen signs work for every row. Nonsingularity makes one row form nonzero, and column divisibility clears anchor denominators. Its algebraic-integral norm gives modulus at least one (at least two after the k=1 parity normalization). Thus the analytic error cannot be small in both of its contributions.
2. The two-entry specialization (1040–1148) bounds coefficient growth by H_2^k and residual decay by R_2^{-k}. It has two distinct outputs. Every integer order k gives an excluded range. Choosing k just above log(Z)/log(R_2) gives the continuous upper bound, subsequently put into the degree-seven logarithmic envelope (1150–1175). The latter has independent small-anchor consumers, so it should not be removed merely because the all-large proof uses integer-order intervals.
3. The three-entry order-two construction (1179–1329) divides different rows by their explicit factors while retaining the root-column divisibility. This reduces the coefficient/residual normalization and leaves determinant -226800 H^3 Delta^10 nonzero. Its bound supplies the M interval, and its coarse stated window is independently used for small triples. These are distinct consumers.
4. The five two-entry finite families (1400–1638) have coefficient degree k and residual order c^{-(m+k)}. N_2 and N_3 use m=k, while the A families use m=k-1. The coefficient formulas, division by row factors and g, determinant constants, and residual/coefficient bounds all remain visible. Their intervals occupy different powers of C and have different anchor dependence. No one family is asserted to dominate another.
5. The four quadratic rows (1640–1790) cancel four inverse powers, giving residual order c^{-5}; together they retain rank four because F_s cannot vanish under C>=200S. Their two terms yield the P interval, with coefficient max(BS^3,B^3S^2), while the adaptive residual retains the ratio B/A. The five linear rows (1792–1852) cancel three inverse powers, have nonzero determinant without extra separation of moduli, and supply the Q interval. The number of anchors, coefficient degree, residual order, and size restrictions differ; these are not redundant variants of a single stated estimate.
6. For six entries all of modulus at least three (1857–2009), structural growth first gives C>A^2 T^5/S^2 and D>G=A^2 C^7/(S^2 T^4). The retained triple-distance bound then yields C>400 A^7 B^5 S^3. These bounds verify the successive forbidden-interval comparisons. The initial finite chain is G,Q,N_2,P; S relative to B^2 determines whether A_3,M are needed before N_3, and S relative to B^3 determines whether N_{3,bs} is needed before A_4. The end is A_4,O_4,A_5,O_5,O_6,... . The ordinary overlap ratio is multiplied by (R_2/H_2)^2>1 at every subsequent order, and the lower endpoints go to infinity. Avoidance then forces D above arbitrarily large endpoints. This also works if an intermediate interval is empty: D above its lower endpoint is already above its upper endpoint, so the next transition is still valid. No separate nonemptiness check is needed.
