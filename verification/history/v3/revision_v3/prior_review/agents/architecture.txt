The genuine proof structure

1. Zero small entries. The fourth-entry gap and retained interpolation gap give C>400 A^7 B^5 S^3 and D>A^2 C^7/(S^2 T^4). Each finite family excludes a closed interval because a nonzero integral form has modulus at least one. The interval chain then reaches the ordinary all-order family, whose later overlaps increase geometrically and whose lower endpoints tend to infinity. This is an infinite-cover contradiction, not a final height enumeration. See 1860–2009.
2. At least two small entries. For exactly two, the analytic argument bounds the third large modulus C by 10^550. Reversible descent makes the bounded nondegenerate-pair graphs complete, while the two degenerate pair types receive separate proofs. The classification permits at most three small entries. For three small entries, at most one bounded further extension exists, and the analytic alternatives or overlapping windows exclude three further entries. See 2024–2880.
3. Exactly one small entry. The five excluded regions bound the first pair, the discriminant argument produces finitely many fields, and S<15 produces 56 triples in addition to (1,3,8). For each fixed triple the interval calculation gives N(t)<N0. This finite complement is eliminated by the irregular-triple inequality and, for the last two rows, a congruence. No fourth-entry enumeration and no He–Togbe–Ziegler theorem is used. See 3044–3847.

Tempting changes to reject

- Do not merge general-three (687–705) with separated-three (707–721). The former allows Q>=P, whereas the latter needs Q>=4P and has both the sharper 3/4 residual and the vanishing-form interval. The two-small cutoff and small-anchor alternative explicitly need the weaker separation regime before they can establish the stronger one.
- Do not merge or delete product-four, product-five and five-frontier-gaps. Their arities and constants differ; the preliminary product gaps also supply hypotheses used by their sharper successors. In particular the frontier proof consumes product-four, so simply replacing the weaker lemma by the stronger one creates a circular justification.
- Do not demote the logarithmic height corollary as a trivial consequence. It is used in three distinct small-anchor arguments and avoids three repetitions of its logarithmic division.
- Do not merge the descent with a particular finite graph lemma. It separately proves completeness for both the radius-10^550 pair graphs and the radius-10^40 triple lists. Keeping zero and anchor coincidences during generation is load-bearing.
- Do not remove the exact finite computation bounds or move all the tables to an appendix merely to reduce pages. The field/pair/triple lists and search radii prove exhaustiveness. The repetition of specialized interval tables at 1919–1933, 2931–2948 and 3633–3649 also performs useful local work: constants, the pointwise relabelled residual and the treatment of powers of A differ. A generic replacement would add parameter bookkeeping and requires a separate exact clause/endpoint map.
- Do not merge the two-small and three-small propositions into sm:main's proof. They are substantial separate case results; sm:main's short assembly tells the reader exactly how they exhaust the branch.
- Do not replace the elementary continued-fraction criterion, exceptional-pair arguments or full-ring {1,3} argument by an outside theorem merely for brevity. Their local hypotheses and full-ring conclusions are part of the self-contained route.
- Do not change font size, font family, margins, packages or macro conventions to match the skill's default house preamble. The author's explicit style governs.
