Comprehension reconstruction
The original object is a hypothetical sextuple in the full imaginary quadratic integer ring. With exactly two small entries a,b, its four large moduli are S<=T<=C<=D. The analytic cutoff proves C<10^550, not a bound on every possible extension independently of a sextuple. Thus three mutually compatible extensions must lie in that disk. For ab+1 nonzero, reversible mutations generate every common extension in the disk from the closed radius-33 seed set. Retaining zero and anchor coincidences is necessary until generation ends. Filtering then gives the printed graphs, in which the three extensions would form a triangle. Zero triangles give the contradiction.

Descent cannot be applied when ab+1=0. The small-pair enumeration isolates {-1,1} and one Eisenstein pair. The first is reduced to axes, then to four real extensions of distinct absolute values in squarefree fields d=7 mod 8. Cubic gaps and the ordinary envelope force d<10^4. The Pell/continued-fraction graph has no triangles below 10^550, again contradicting the cutoff. The Eisenstein transformation proves every common extension purely imaginary, with at most one on each half-axis, so that pair cannot occur in a sextuple.

The small-triple classification proves there are never four small entries. For three small entries, each table row has at most one further extension in the closed radius-10^40 disk, and that extension has modulus below 85. If all three additional entries lie outside, coherent-form alternatives force incompatible quadratic/quintic growth when compared with the ordinary upper bound. If one lies inside, it becomes a fourth anchor. Four-anchor nonvanishing first forces the other two entries above the lower forbidden endpoint. The four-anchor order-one interval overlaps the three-anchor order-two interval, and together they cover the ordinary height upper bound. This excludes the other possible sextuple. The final assembly correctly exhausts exactly two or exactly three small entries.
