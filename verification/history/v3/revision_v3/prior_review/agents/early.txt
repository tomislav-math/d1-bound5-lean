Fresh comprehension reconstruction
This reconstruction was made from the manuscript before consulting revision_v2/agents/structural.md.

The original objects are distinct nonzero algebraic integers with pairwise products plus one equal to integral squares. The proof is about their complex moduli. Moduli may coincide, and an allowed pairwise square may be zero. The norm gives the one quantitative integrality fact used repeatedly: a nonzero algebraic integer has modulus at least one. The full integer ring matters in both this fact and the finite basis enumerations.

Section 2 first turns irregularity of a triple into a size gap. The two regular-completion expressions have nonzero integral product for an irregular triple. Bounding their sum and product forces a quadratic majorant to be nonnegative. Its negative endpoint values exclude the entire interval from B to 2AB. Next the last triple of any quadruple whose second entry has modulus at least three is shown to be irregular. For C>=4, regularity would permit compatible square-root choices whose integral difference u+v−w has modulus below one. Vanishing of this difference then gives the impossible equation 2(ar−uv)=1. For 3<=B<=C<4, integral norms bound all relevant fields and entries, allowing the explicit finite check.

The matrix of chosen roots is then separated into a rank-one contribution and a bounded error. In even dimension its integral determinant is nonzero by reduction modulo two. Upper bounds on that determinant therefore force the largest entry to dominate the product of preceding moduli. For five entries, the proof separately establishes nonsingularity of the Schur complement, so it does not apply the even-dimensional argument improperly. A more exact cubic expansion improves the four-entry factor. The endpoint comparison is convex in the normalized fourth modulus and retains equality cases.

Section 3 fixes two, three or four tuple entries and compares the two later entries p,q through their reciprocals. The quantities z and R_i are integral despite their analytic definitions. Multiplying the interpolation coefficients by the Vandermonde product cancels their denominators. The weighted sum cancels the nonconstant degrees below m, leaving the explicit term 1−f(xy) and the tail F_m. The latter is bounded by an absolutely convergent coefficient series. Nonzero forms have modulus at least one, and the resulting sum of a term decreasing with |z| and a term increasing with |z| excludes intermediate ranges. Vanishing cannot simply be ignored. The two-anchor form gives a regular-completion identity and a small upper bound for Q, the three- and four-anchor forms give bounded vanishing ranges, and two overlapping three-anchor forms cannot both vanish under the stated separation.

The retained-gap lemma is the main assembly. Two applications of the two-anchor estimate first make C large enough to contradict the upper vanishing range of the three-anchor form on a,b,s. Its nonzero modulus bound then yields the fifth-power lower bound for C. Repeating this five-entry argument with c,d yields the fifth-power bound for D. That bound makes the four-anchor form nonzero, whose estimate yields the seventh-power bound for D. The additional size consequences prepare the later simultaneous-approximation constructions. The argument for C uses only five entries, so its repetition is not circular.
