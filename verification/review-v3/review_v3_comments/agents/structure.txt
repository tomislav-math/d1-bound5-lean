12. The second bound in Lemma 2.3 can be strengthened
----------------------------------------------------
The reviewer is right. Lemma 2.2 applies to the second assertion too,
giving D>2BC>=6C and D>18. Hence
  A^2+B^2+C^2+D^2 < (13/12)D^2.
Even keeping the current bound ||E||_2<1/8 yields
  |det N| < (325/512)(13/12+(5/8)/324)D^2
          = (914225/1327104)D^2.
The integral determinant bound ABCD<=|det N| then gives
  D > (1327104/914225)ABC > (29/20)ABC.
Thus 1.36 is a valid but unnecessarily weak proposed coefficient.

A cleaner improvement uses the already displayed weighted row sums
1/9 and 11/96 without rounding their maximum up to 1/8.
The entrywise majorant is a symmetric nonnegative matrix, so its spectral
radius bounds ||E||_2 and is at most its maximum weighted row sum. Therefore
  ||E||_2 < 11/96.
Put s=1/2+11/96=59/96 and h=3/2+11/96=155/96.
Exactly the existing singular-value and rank-one argument gives
  |det N| < hs^2(s+sum A_i^2)
          < hs^2(13/12+s/324)D^2
          = (18212679025/27518828544)D^2
          < (2/3)D^2.
The last strict inequality has exact margin
  2/3-18212679025/27518828544
    =133206671/27518828544 >0.
Consequently the simple strengthened conclusion D>(3/2)ABC is valid.
No analytic assertion beyond those already used in the proof is needed.

Recommendation: this is a genuine, low-cost optional improvement. If
improving the statement, use the clean 3/2 instead of decimal 1.36, replace
only this proof segment, and leave subsequent calculations at their
existing weaker constants. Every previous use of D>(3/8)ABC remains valid.
It does not itself shorten the main argument or reduce the finite graphs,
so do not undertake a new global numerical optimization solely for it.

17. Notation: several actual conflicts remain, but scope matters
--------------------------------------------------------------
The review's field-basis complaint is still present in the frozen v3:
* Introduction, lines 95--98: omega^2-tau omega+nu=0 and Delta=4nu-tau^2.
* Lemma 2.2, lines 210--218: omega^2=t omega-h and Delta=4h-t^2.
* End of section 5.2, lines 1644--1645: the introduction's tau,nu are used.
* Section 8.3, lines 3203--3235: omega^2=t omega-u, with t and u then
  appearing in ring multiplication, norm, disk enumeration, and orbit rule.
* Section 8.5, lines 3523--3531: t is still the basis trace in the square
  test, despite t denoting the fourth tuple entry elsewhere nearby.

Simply changing every field-basis t to tau is unsafe for readability:
Proposition 8.4 uses tau=59/20, and section 5.3 uses
tau=21/(1024*999). The field discriminant also appears explicitly in
Lemma 8.2 and Lemma 8.3. Recommended coherent field notation is therefore
  omega, tau_K, nu_K, Delta_K
with omega^2-tau_K omega+nu_K=0. It avoids introducing a new local/global
collision when the reviewer suggestion is implemented. The field
parameter d may remain in the enumeration sections; the q in Lemma 2.2
was introduced because that proof already uses d for a tuple member.
There is no reason to rename every tuple member d across the manuscript.

Actual Delta uses:
* Delta as field discriminant: intro, finite tail check, pair (1,3),
  field reduction, enumeration and square test.
* Delta_m as a Vandermonde product: section 3, introduced at line 539.
* Unsubscripted Delta as a Vandermonde product: sections 4 and 7.1.
* Delta as the five numerical determinant constants: section 5.2,
  explicitly scoped by the sentence at line 1415.

Minimal remedy: use Delta_K for the field discriminant and a distinct
symbol, e.g. c_det, for the five numerical determinant constants. Retain
Delta_m and the locally defined Delta for Vandermonde products. A full
Vandermonde rename throughout all polynomials is not needed to remove the
real ambiguity. If editorial uniformity is preferred, Delta_m can be
used consistently for the m-anchor Vandermonde, but this is a larger
mechanical change requiring formula-by-formula correspondence checks.

K really denotes both the field and scalar coefficients. There are small
local scalar occurrences K=400 in section 6 and K=B^2S^4T^6 in section 7.1,
as well as K for retained-gap coefficients in sections 8.4 and 9.1.
The first two could harmlessly become kappa_0 and K_4. The latter K is an
explicit local scalar with substantial table use; retaining it is not a
mathematical ambiguity when its definition is repeated. Avoid introducing
many new glyphs just to enforce globally unique names for every local h,
F, Q, k, H. In particular H=32 or 48 is explicitly a row parameter,
whereas H as an anchor product and H as a gap coefficient have separate
local definitions. A rename of the 32/48 parameter to H_0 is possible,
but H_0 is already used for the ordinary tail later, so it is not an
automatic global improvement.

Recommendation: do the field-basis/discriminant cleanup and numerical
determinant-constant rename first. Use a compact conventions paragraph
(or at most a short table) listing only recurring global symbols:
K, O_K, N(z), capital moduli, omega, tau_K, nu_K, Delta_K,
and Vandermonde products. Do not add an encyclopedic list of every local
constant or rename all uses of H,h,K,F,Q,k in one pass.

18. Computational reproducibility
---------------------------------
The abstract (lines 26--27) says "complete search bounds and output tables"
are included. The introduction (lines 111--112) says "complete output
tables". The manuscript does include enumeration procedures and result
tables, but it does not print all graph vertices/edges or all 1016
per-field outputs. The phrase "complete output tables" invites a stronger
reading than the actual contents support.

Suggested abstract wording:
  "The remaining cases are reduced to finite computations. We give
  explicit search bounds and enumeration procedures, together with
  tables of the resulting counts and cutoff constants."
Suggested final conventions wording:
  "The finite-sum formulas, bounded enumeration procedures, and tables
  of counts and cutoff constants are included in the article."
An availability sentence with an actual permanent URL can be added once
the computation archive is publicly deposited. Do not claim a public
repository exists before it does.

The reviewer subsequently reconstructed all counts from the descriptions,
so the stronger claim that they are impossible to verify without the
authors' code is not supported. The mathematical algorithms are described;
publishing executable implementations makes verification far easier and
reduces transcription risk. The assertion that a journal will "almost
certainly" require code is speculative without a target journal policy.

Recommendation: use the existing exact verification packages as the basis
of a single frozen reproducibility archive. Include the canonical TeX hash,
script-to-result mapping, dependencies, command order, expected exact
outputs, and an unambiguous runner. The newly claimed external ZIP has not
been supplied to this agent; its stated contents must not be treated as
locally inspected evidence. The author's own available checks and the
reviewer's new independent package should remain distinguishable.
