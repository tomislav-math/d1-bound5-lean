1. The f_3 estimate: accept the need for an explicit proof, but use a substantially simpler proof
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The existing estimate 45/1024 <= P^3 |f_3| <= 85/1024 is correct. Its omission is a presentation gap, with important downstream consequences, rather than a false mathematical statement. There is an elementary two-line proof giving precisely the printed constants.

Set w=y/x, rho=|w|<=1/4. Since |x|=1/P,

  16 P^3 |f_3| = |1-w| |1-w^2|.

The ordinary and reverse triangle inequalities therefore give

  (1-rho)(1-rho^2) <= 16 P^3 |f_3|
                         <= (1+rho)(1+rho^2).

The left side decreases and the right side increases on 0<=rho<=1/4. At rho=1/4 they are 45/64 and 85/64. This proves exactly the existing bounds, without differentiation, extrema analysis, square roots, or any numerical approximation.

Suggested replacement for the current unsupported sentence:

  For three anchors, put $w=y/x$. Since $|w|\le1/4$ and
  $16P^3|f_3|=|1-w||1-w^2|$, the triangle inequalities give
  \[
   \frac{45}{64}=(1-1/4)(1-1/16)
   \le16P^3|f_3|
   \le(1+1/4)(1+1/16)=\frac{85}{64}.
  \]
  Put ...

An inline version of the same calculation is also sufficient.

The reviewer's g(c) argument is valid, including its endpoint minimum and interior upper bound. It is simply unnecessary. Its g'(c) is correct. The critical point -s/6 is a maximum when it belongs to [-rho,rho]; otherwise the maximum occurs at an endpoint. The universal upper bound 32s^3/27 remains valid in both situations. The factorization above is shorter and gives the paper's particular constants directly.

The same estimate applies in Lemma 3.4 without any further proof. Important notational distinction: the index 1000 in r_1000, l_1000, u_1000 there comes from AP>=1000, because A>=1 and P>=1000. Its ratio |y/x| is still only bounded by 1/4. It is not assumed <=1/1000. The factorized estimate is valid for any rho<1 and would also give bounds for rho<=1/1000, but those sharper bounds must not be silently used in Lemma 3.4.

The companion script checks every rational comparison for H=32,48,1000. It also checks Uh_0<18 for H=32,48. Thus adding this proof closes the omitted justification while preserving every downstream constant, including the tight nonvanishing step in Lemma 3.7.

11. Removing maximum modulus and Hadamard: the proposed weakening is valid, but there is a better local alternative
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The existing maximum-modulus/Hadamard proof of the factor 16 is mathematically sound. Keeping it is a defensible choice: it is short and does not invoke a deep theorem. However, if the aim is an elementary proof suitable for later formalization, the following alternative is preferable to the reviewer's factor 64.

Expand the 4x4 Vandermonde determinant. It contains 24 monomials. For A<=B<=S<=T, each monomial has modulus at most B S^2 T^3, by the elementary rearrangement inequality (or by interchanging two inverted exponents). Therefore

  |Delta_4| <= 24 B S^2 T^3.

No complex maximum principle or Hadamard inequality is used. The script checks all 24 exponent comparisons independently after writing A=u, B=uv, S=uvw, T=uvwz with v,w,z>=1.

With exactly the existing r,l,u in Lemma 3.6, exact arithmetic gives

  24*101/199 < 13,
  2400*u/99 = 0.9606367985... < 49/50.

Thus the direct part of Lemma 3.6 may be replaced by

  |L_4| < 13 B S^2 T^3/Z + (49/50) S T^2 Z/(A P^4).

The assertion for L_4=0, namely 10ABSTP^3<Q<17ABSTP^3, stays exactly the same, because it does not depend on the Vandermonde factor.

Crucially, this local weakening need not weaken the retained gap. Before the final L_4 argument, Lemma 3.7 already proves C>A^2 T^5/S^2 and T>2ABS. They imply

  C/(BST) > A^2 T^4/(BS^3) > 16 A^6 B^3 S >= 16,

so C>16BST. With k=3999/4000, Z=|sqrt(cd+1)| and the already established D>A^2 C^5/S^2,

  (13BS^2T^3/Z)^2
  < 169 B^2 S^6 T^6/(k A^2 C^6)
  < 169/(16^6 k A^2 B^4)
  <= 169/(16^6 k) < 1/10000.

The first summand is therefore less than 1/100. Nonvanishing gives

  Z > (99/98) A C^4/(ST^2).

Putting X=A^2C^8/(S^2T^4), one has X>=C^2>10^6. Hence

  Z^2-1 > X,

because ((99/98)^2-1)*10^6>1. This proves the ORIGINAL bound

  D>A^2 C^7/(S^2T^4).

It is important to adjust one other direct use of Lemma 3.6: the final subcase of the two-small-entry cutoff lemma, source label sm:bs7-cutoff, at current lines 2306-2312. Its display becomes

Every statement of a retained gap, every forbidden-interval formula, all overlap margins, and all N_0 values remain unchanged. It is an optional substantive simplification, not a needed correction.
