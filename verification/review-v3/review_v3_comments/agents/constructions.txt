COMMENT 13: ONE ROOT-TRANSFER PROOF

The two existing arguments are equivalent. In Lemma 4.1, let

 theta^2 = 1+1/(xc),
 u = +/- r_x v_x/(xz),
 z^2 = cd+1.

The assumptions ensure theta != 0 and z != 0. The algebraic identity is

 u^2/theta^2 = 1+(c-x)/(xz^2).

Choose a square root w of the right side with Re(w)>=0, and choose the sign
of u so that u=theta*w. Since |w+1|>=1,

 |u-theta| = |theta| |w-1|
            <= |theta| |(c-x)/(xz^2)|
            <= C/(|x| Z^2) sqrt(1+1/(|x| C)) (1+|x|/C).

This argument requires neither D>=C nor XC>=1000 nor C>=4Y. It applies in
Lemma 4.1 under its original C>1 and AC>1 assumptions. It also remains valid
when r_x or v_x vanishes: then w=0, and no division by those roots occurs.
The signs are chosen once for each fixed entry and hence are consistent
across all approximation rows.

The statement |theta+u|>=|theta| used in the current earlier proof follows
from the same choice, because |theta+u|=|theta| |1+w|. Conversely, the
earlier sign choice can also be justified immediately by the parallelogram
identity. Neither argument is mathematically stronger than the other.

Concrete revision:

* Put the w proof above at the first occurrence in Lemma 4.1. Keep the final
  monotonicity argument e(A_j)<=e(S), since that is needed there.
* At the beginning of Section 5.1, retain the displayed pointwise error
  estimate and say that it follows from the sign choice in Lemma 4.1.
* Retain the local deductions e=3/2 and e=101/100 and the explanation that
  roots are not divided out. The exact inequalities used are
  (1001/1000)(5/4)^2 < (3/2)^2 and
  (1001/1000)(201/200)^2 < (101/100)^2.

This removes actual duplicate reasoning without adding a separate lemma or
new global notation. It does not require weakening any assumption.

COMMENT 14: GENERAL POLE ORDERS

The common analytic calculation can indeed be written once. For distinct
nodes alpha_0,...,alpha_r, positive integral orders p_0,...,p_r, and P=sum p_j,
consider

 I_i = (1/(2*pi*i)) integral_Gamma
       (1+w/N)^(k+1/2) / ((w-alpha_i) product_j(w-alpha_j)^p_j) dw.

Assume |alpha_j/N| <= 1-eta with eta>0 and P>k+1/2. The same branch-cut
deformation gives

 |I_i| <= |N|^(-P) eta^(-(P-k-1/2))
          Gamma(k+3/2) Gamma(P-k-1/2)/(pi Gamma(P+1)).

Leibniz's rule also gives a common residue-coefficient formula with summation
condition sum h_l=p_j+delta_ij-1 and factors

 binom(k+1/2,h_j) N^(-k)(N+alpha_j)^(k-h_j)
 product_{l != j} binom(-p_l-delta_il,h_l)
                    (alpha_j-alpha_l)^(-p_l-delta_il-h_l).

For Lemma 4.1, P=(m+1)k. For Section 5.2, P=m+2k. These substitutions recover
the two residual bounds exactly before their different clearing and row
normalizations. Thus the proposed unification is correct at the contour and
residue-calculus level.

It is not automatically an arithmetic unification. In particular:

* General p_j can exceed k, producing negative powers of N+alpha_j. The
  existing polynomial integrality proof explicitly relies on h_j<=k.
* A concrete example is k=1, nodes (0,1,2), orders (1,2,1), i=j=1. The above
  residue coefficient satisfies

     N f_11 = -(N+1)-3/(8(N+1)).

  There is an uncancelled pole at N=-1. Clearing only powers of N and node
  differences, as in the present construction, does not make it polynomial.
  An unrestricted general-order integrality assertion would therefore be
  false in the present normalization.
* The finite construction has row divisions by x-y, x and y, followed by
  division by g. Its column divisibility and exact integer constants depend
  on these divisions.
* Lemma 4.1 uses the all-orders Bennett determinant identity. Section 5.2
  proves five specific determinant identities through a two-by-two block,
  and permits a c^(k-m) factor. A generic contour bound supplies no generic
  determinant formula or rank result for these arithmetic normalizations.
* The coefficient table, the g divisibility, and the specialized M bounds
  still need their current finite computations after analytic unification.

Recommendation: do not introduce a broad new arithmetic theorem during this
revision merely to reduce repetition. If desired, state the common contour
bound and residue differentiation once, then specialize it with explicit
normalizations in each construction. Keep the determinant and integrality
arguments separate and transparent. The expected saving is modest, rather
than the removal of most of Section 5.2. A larger refactor should have its
own exact equivalence review and must state the p_j restrictions explicitly.

COMMENT 15: INTERVAL CHAINS

The paper already has two levels of shared machinery:

* (ff-window) converts an arithmetic row inequality to a forbidden interval.
  It is not an interval-chain lemma.
* Section 8.1, entitled 'A common interval calculation', supplies one raw
  endpoint table, a sufficient overlap table, and the ordinary-tail argument.
  It is used both for the five unbounded regions and for the S<15 reduction.

There is nevertheless genuine duplication. The raw interval tables in
Sections 6, 8.1 and 9.1 have the same monomial pattern. Section 6 specializes
the constants. Section 9 discards positive powers of A from upper endpoints,
which is equivalent to substituting A=1 in those endpoints and in the
ordinary upper base. Lower endpoints have no A factors and do not change.
Its M_bs is separately specialized, but that is already a parameter in the
Section 8.1 table.

A concrete useful consolidation is as follows.

1. Place the common raw endpoint table after the approximation constructions,
   before Section 6, with the size assumptions under which every row is
   available. For example the relevant simultaneous guards are A>=1,
   C>=max(1000/A,200S,4T), D>=C, together with the distinct-entry hypotheses
   and the specified valid M and R constants. Actual applications have
   strict stronger versions supplied by the retained-gap lemma.
2. Section 6 supplies its uniform constants and cites that table, rather than
   reproducing all ten endpoint rows.
3. Section 8 supplies the region or pair constants and continues to use the
   table as now.
4. Section 9 says explicitly that it uses the same table after replacing A
   by 1 only in upper endpoints, and supplies its fixed-pair M values and
   specialized M_bs. Its duplicate ten-row raw endpoint table can disappear.
5. Keep Section 9's table of c_e x^(p_e). Its gap is C>K T^5/S^2 and the
   variable to be bounded is x=N(t). Its selected chain always uses
   P,A_3,M,N_3,N_{3,bs},A_4. These are materially different substitutions
   from the G/rho calculation and should remain visible.

There is a further verified saving in Section 6. The existing Section 8.1
overlap table works there with G=400 and rho=400/(35/32), and the appropriate
uniform M values. The first two entries of that table use T>2BS rather than
the stronger T>2ABS used in Section 6, giving A^10 rather than A^20 in the
first comparison, and A^8 rather than A^16 in the second. Even these weaker
entries are strictly greater than one at A=B=S=3. All later entries agree
after rho=400/R_bs is substituted. The exact checker verified all fifteen
common-table comparisons for this use. Consequently the full overlap table
can also be shared between Sections 6 and 8 if moved before its first use.
Section 6 can retain one displayed critical quotient and state that the
others exceed their chosen exact lower bounds.

The chain principle itself is very short. If D avoids every closed interval
[L_j,U_j], D>L_0, U_j>=L_(j+1), and L_j tends to infinity, then induction gives
D>U_j and D>L_(j+1) for all j, which is impossible. This remains valid when
an intermediate interval is empty: D>L_j>U_j already gives the induction.
For the ordinary tail, L_j=a H^(2j), U_j=b R^(2j), with R>H>1. The ratio
U_j/L_(j+1) increases by (R/H)^2, so one checked overlap starts the entire
tail. This elementary statement can be proved once in a short paragraph or
lemma, without an array of extra G,rho,M,R parameters in its statement.

Thus I support the goal of comment 15, in this precise scope. Merely adding
an abstract chain lemma while retaining all duplicate tables would save
little. Replacing all branch-specific inequalities by one heavily
parametrized lemma would hide rather than clarify the genuinely different
growth estimates. The common endpoint table plus the existing shared
overlap calculation achieves the main saving with no new mathematical input.

In Proposition 4.2, A is a positive modulus, so

 27 A B (A C-1)^2/(16 A^2 delta^2)
   = 27 B (A C-1)^2/(16 A delta^2).
