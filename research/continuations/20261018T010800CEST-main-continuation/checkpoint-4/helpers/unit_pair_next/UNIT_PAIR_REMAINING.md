# Remaining general unit-pair exclusion

This is a dependency map from the actual frozen manuscript, not a claim of a completed unit-pair proof. The coordinator is the sole compiler and remote writer. In particular, the exceptional Eisenstein pair is a different pair from {-1,1}; its new complete coordinate proof does not settle the general unit pair.

## Shortest faithful reduction

1. **Axis/Pell classification, still missing.** In the full quadratic integer basis write 2r=U+V sqrt(-d), 2s=X+Y sqrt(-d). From r²+s²=2 derive U²+X²-d(V²+Y²)=8 and UV+XY=0. Extract a primitive direction (U,X)=g(p,q); integer orthogonality gives (V,Y)=h(q,-p), so (p²+q²)(g²-dh²)=8. Coprimality rules out sums4 and8, leaving1 and2. Coordinate parity forces g,h even in both full-ring branches, including the half-integral branch. This yields exactly z=±(dv²+1), u²-dv²=2, or z=±2uv sqrt(-d), u²-dv²=1, with v nonzero. Hence every nonzero extension has modulus at least d+1. The integer primitive-vector lemma and its half-integral parity transfer must actually be proved; the symmetric-five obstruction does not substitute for them.
2. **Elementary eliminations, partly supplied.** d=1 admits no nonzero extension. For d>1, two imaginary extensions on the same half-axis are incompatible by the shared-field negative-integer-square criterion modulo d; thus there are at most two imaginary extensions. Real and imaginary extensions cannot coexist: conjugation supplies both signs of the mixed product, and the six root conditions contradict the new `discrete_unit_pair_symmetric_obstruction`. The latter is already drafted without any artificial tuple enlargement. Existing full-ring rational-square and coordinate-conjugation APIs support the transfer, but these wrappers are not yet written.
3. **Parity, still missing.** For even d, every positive real-extension magnitude is3 modulo4, so at most one extension of each sign can coexist. For odd d, a real extension forces d≡7 modulo8. These are direct integer Pell-congruence arguments. Four real extensions must have distinct absolute values, by the symmetric-five obstruction.
4. **Analytic reduction, still missing.** For the remaining odd d≡7 modulo8, the manuscript's unit cubic gap D>C³/2 (C≥9 and d_entry≠-c_entry) plus the ordinary all-order height envelope forces the smallest extension magnitude S<10^4. Axis classification then gives7≤d<10^4. The unit cubic gap uses a separate second-order square-root cancellation u+v−2z; it is not the currently proved two-anchor gap, whose second anchor modulus must be at least3. The all-order Bennett determinant/integrality drafts do not themselves establish the required height envelope. The general two-small cutoff is also needed later to ensure the third large modulus is at most10^550.
5. **Finite Pell completeness and graph checks, still missing.** Exactly the squarefree d in [7,10000) with d≡7 modulo8 remain. The manuscript reports1016 such fields; that count is not a premise needed by the theorem. For each field enumerate u²-dv²=2 with u²-1≤10^550 by continued fractions. Add both signed magnitudes. Prove the resulting finite compatibility graph has no triangle (the manuscript reports that all edges are opposite pairs). With four extensions, the cutoff puts three within the certified disk, yielding the contradiction.

## A viable kernel certificate implementation

The pinned mathlib already has the genuine proved Legendre theorem:
`Real.exists_rat_eq_convergent {q : ℚ} (h : |xi-q| < 1/(2*q.den^2)) : ∃ n, q=xi.convergent n`
in `Mathlib/NumberTheory/DiophantineApproximation/Basic.lean`, lines534–549. Its source was read, not merely a declaration name. `NumberTheory/DiophantineApproximation/ContinuedFractions.lean` connects this with general continued-fraction convergents. Thus Legendre's theorem need not be reproved. The actual `NumberTheory/Pell.lean` is for norm-one solutions and does not directly classify the required norm-two solutions.

A finite certificate should store, per d, the integer complete-quotient states (m,Q,a), convergent numerators/denominators, and the retained norm-two solutions. Check the displayed recurrence using exact arithmetic, positivity, and exact divisibility. The finite-real bridge proves that each stored state represents (sqrt(d)+m)/Q and that a is its floor; floor bounds can be verified by squared integer comparisons. A generic bridge then identifies stored convergents with `Real.convergent`. The initial state uses the certified integer square root of d.

For an actual norm-two solution, gcd(u,v)=1 follows because its square divides2. The exact Pell identity gives the Legendre approximation bound, since d≥7. Thus its reduced fraction is a genuine convergent. The certificate stops after the first numerator exceeding floor(sqrt(10^550+1)); proved monotonicity of continued-fraction numerators excludes every later index. This stopping/completeness argument is essential: checking a list of Pell solutions alone does not prove exhaustiveness.

Store concrete finite integer lists and use kernel-checked Boolean equalities, following the existing pair-closure architecture. Split certificates by d, with smaller helper chunks if compilation is expensive. For each candidate edge, apply the exact quadratic square checker in model tau=1,nu=(d+1)/4 (the full ring for these d), or the already-proved shared-field rational-square criterion. Checking that every edge joins opposite vertices gives a matching graph and immediately excludes a triangle. Counts19612 vertices/9806 edges/0 triangles may be retained as audits, but are not a substitute for the per-field completeness and graph propositions.

No brute-force enumeration of v to10^275 is needed. A norm-one-unit orbit method would need a separately proved complete norm-two seed classification; choosing an arbitrary Pell unit and sampling powers is not an adequate replacement for the continued-fraction completeness bridge.
